# A simple presentation explanation

“I built a local agent-based simulation of a network. Agents know the rules, but
must investigate which conditions currently hold. One agent may find a reference
while another gains the source needed to use it. They exchange evidence and work
together. A defender can change a service or remove access, so an earlier plan can
stop working. The system compares a leader-directed hierarchy with peer-coordinated
swarm agents under the same resources. Programmed controllers and local LLMs use
the same private evidence and action rules. The goal is to measure investigation,
coordination, recovery, success and cost—not to guarantee that one policy wins.”

Show the application in this order: a paused recipe/team configuration; private
known hosts versus researcher truth; a probe and recorded evidence; organization
links/messages; Patch contradiction or Restore loss/recovery; actual target hold;
saved replay and checksummed export; idle experiment preparation/freeze/queue.
Use the source-identified four worked examples for predictable demonstrations.

What is demonstrated: bounded simulator mechanics, legal capabilities, truthful
UI, equal-information interfaces, accounting, saved configurations and reproducible
software tooling. What is still research: supervisor/expert realism review,
calibrated difficulty, successful live-model qualification, scientific held-out
organization results and publication novelty. No real systems are attacked.

The computers/infrastructure are abstract; the investigation and adaptation
dependencies are explicit model assumptions. An auth probe reveals allowed
methods, not whether an account is weak. A note/reference may be outdated or wrong.
Messages transfer verified evidence, not another agent's session or privilege.
Unchanged incompatible attempts keep failing. Our model has uncertainty from
incomplete evidence and changing conditions, not a random compatibility coin flip.
